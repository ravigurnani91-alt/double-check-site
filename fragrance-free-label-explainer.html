CASE 0004

What "Fragrance-Free" Actually Means on a Skincare Label

Explainer · Ingredients · 5 min read
FLAGGED

If your skin turns red, stings, or reacts to almost everything you put on it, you already know how exhausting the search for a product that won't fight back can be.

The Claim

"Fragrance-free" and "unscented" are used across skincare labels as if they mean the same, safer thing for sensitive skin. They don't — and neither term is strictly regulated.

What Each Label Actually Permits

LabelVerdictWhat It Actually Means
"Fragrance-Free" MIXED Means no materials were added specifically to create a scent. It does not mean the product is free of scented ingredients entirely — an ingredient like an essential oil can still be present if it's serving another stated function, such as an emollient, and can still trigger allergic reactions in sensitive users.
"Unscented" FLAGGED Means the product has no perceptible smell — not that it's free of fragrance chemicals. Ironically, this is often achieved by adding masking fragrance compounds specifically to neutralize the natural odor of other ingredients, meaning "unscented" products can contain more added fragrance chemicals than "fragrance-free" ones.
Regulatory status (US) FLAGGED Neither term has an official legal definition in US cosmetics regulation. Skincare products are regulated as cosmetics, not drugs, which carries a lighter regulatory bar — so brands can apply "fragrance-free" and "unscented" according to their own internal standards, not a shared legal one.

Case File Verdict

If you're avoiding fragrance for sensitive skin or allergies, "fragrance-free" is generally the safer of the two labels to look for — but it's not a guarantee, since naturally-scented ingredients like essential oils can still be present. "Unscented" is the label to be more cautious of: the absence of smell can come from added masking chemicals, which is the opposite of what most people assume the word means. Either way, the actual ingredient list is more reliable than the front-of-bottle label.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is fragrance-free the same as unscented?

No. Fragrance-free means no fragrance materials or masking scents were added for the purpose of creating a scent. Unscented means the product has no perceptible smell, which is often achieved by adding masking fragrance chemicals to neutralize the odor of other ingredients — meaning unscented products can contain more added fragrance chemicals than fragrance-free ones.

Can a fragrance-free product still cause an allergic reaction?

Yes. Fragrance-free products can still contain ingredients like essential oils that have a scent but serve another stated purpose (such as an emollient), and these can still trigger allergic reactions in sensitive individuals.

Are "fragrance-free" and "unscented" legally regulated terms?

In the US, neither term has an official legal definition under cosmetics regulation, since the FDA treats most skincare products as cosmetics rather than drugs, which are less strictly regulated. This means brands can apply these labels according to their own internal standards.
